## Make the rubric explicit

A constitution is a collection of principles such as helpfulness, honesty, or non-harm. The model is asked to inspect a candidate answer against a principle and then propose a revision. The process turns a vague alignment target into text that can be shown, debated, and revised.

## Critique is a generated label

The critique itself may be produced by a model, so it is not automatically neutral or correct. Principles can conflict, prompts can be ambiguous, and a model can learn to recite the rubric without changing its behavior. The method changes where supervision enters; it does not remove the need for evaluation.

## Separate policy from proof

Use the mitigation toggles to compare an unreviewed response with a critique-and-revision path. Ask which failure modes the principle addresses and which remain outside its wording. A constitution is a steering instrument, not a formal guarantee of safety.

$$
\text{response} \rightarrow \text{critique} \rightarrow \text{revision}
$$

## Recap

The central idea is **Constitutional AI**. The implementation detail matters because it changes what the model can learn, how much memory or compute the system needs, or how confidently we can interpret its behavior. Keep the equation visible, name the controlled variables, and treat every benchmark or safeguard as part of the model’s operating definition.
