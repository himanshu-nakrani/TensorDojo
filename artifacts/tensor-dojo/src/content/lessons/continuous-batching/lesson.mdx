## Generation is an uneven workload

Training batches are usually rectangular: every example contributes the same number of positions to a step. Autoregressive serving is ragged. One request may finish after ten tokens while another continues for a thousand. A static batch either pads the short request or waits for the long one.

## Admit work as slots open

Continuous batching treats active sequences as a changing set. When one request finishes, another can enter the batch at a safe scheduling boundary. The server must track per-request state, fair scheduling, maximum context, and cancellation without corrupting the shared attention computation.

## Latency and throughput are different objectives

A scheduler that maximizes tokens per second may delay a short request; a scheduler that minimizes tail latency may leave compute unused. Use occupancy, remaining tokens, and arrival rate to identify which quantity improves when batch occupancy rises, and which quantity can get worse.

$$
\text{throughput} = \frac{\text{generated tokens}}{\text{wall-clock time}}
$$

## Recap

The central idea is **continuous batching**: when a short request finishes, its slot can admit a new one instead of sitting idle until the longest request in a static batch completes. Occupancy, remaining tokens, and arrival rate are different knobs — throughput tracks occupied slots, while p50/p99 tell you whether packing the batch is costing tail latency.
