## Raw tokens can lie

A dataset with ten copies of the same document contains ten times the storage, but not ten times the information. Training sees the repeated sequence again and again, so the model can lower its loss by memorizing that exact text. A raw token count therefore overstates the diversity of the evidence presented to the optimizer.

## Exact and near duplicates

Exact deduplication can hash normalized documents. Near-duplicate detection is harder: two pages may share a long passage while differing in navigation, timestamps, or formatting. In practice, systems compare shingles or embeddings and remove one member of a highly similar cluster. The goal is not to erase all repetition—language itself repeats—but to prevent accidental corpus amplification.

## Connect it to generalization

Use the lab to increase the number of repeated examples and observe how a learner can appear to improve while becoming less representative of unseen data. This is the same tension as overfitting in a small neural network, now expressed at corpus scale.

$$
\text{effective examples} \leq \text{raw examples}
$$

## Recap

The central idea is **Data deduplication**. The implementation detail matters because it changes what the model can learn, how much memory or compute the system needs, or how confidently we can interpret its behavior. Keep the equation visible, name the controlled variables, and treat every benchmark or safeguard as part of the model’s operating definition.
