## Fluency is a local objective

Next-token prediction rewards continuations that look plausible in context. A plausible continuation can be false, especially when the prompt asks for a detail not represented in the model’s learned distribution. The model can produce a confident sentence without having consulted a source.

## Grounding adds a checkable path

Retrieval-augmented generation supplies documents or records at inference time. The generator can condition on that evidence, cite it, or be instructed to abstain when the evidence is missing. Retrieval helps, but irrelevant or contradictory documents can still lead to a persuasive error.

## Evaluate claims, not just style

A grounded system needs claim-level evaluation: is the answer supported, is the citation relevant, and did the response omit an important qualification? Use the explorer to compare a fluent answer with a supported answer and name the evidence that would change your confidence.

$$
p(y \mid x, D) \propto p(y \mid x, \text{retrieved evidence }D)
$$

## Recap

The central idea is **Hallucination and grounding**. The implementation detail matters because it changes what the model can learn, how much memory or compute the system needs, or how confidently we can interpret its behavior. Keep the equation visible, name the controlled variables, and treat every benchmark or safeguard as part of the model’s operating definition.
