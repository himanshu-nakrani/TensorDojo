## A model can be the labeler

RLAIF replaces or supplements human preference labels with feedback from an evaluator model. The evaluator receives a prompt, candidate responses, and a rubric, then produces a comparison or score. This can increase throughput and make a rubric more consistent across a large dataset.

## Scale does not equal objectivity

An evaluator model can prefer verbosity, mimic the style of its own training data, or miss subtle factual and safety failures. Prompt formatting and candidate order can influence judgments. Agreement between two model judges is not the same as agreement with people affected by the system.

## Use human checks strategically

The strongest workflow mixes scalable AI feedback with targeted human audits, adversarial examples, and disagreement sampling. The goal is not to choose humans or models in the abstract; it is to spend human attention where the automated judge is least trustworthy.

## Recap

The central idea is **RLAIF**. The implementation detail matters because it changes what the model can learn, how much memory or compute the system needs, or how confidently we can interpret its behavior. Keep the equation visible, name the controlled variables, and treat every benchmark or safeguard as part of the model’s operating definition.
